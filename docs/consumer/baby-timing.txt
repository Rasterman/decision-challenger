# Now or in Two Years: The Timing Question

# Note on data: NYC infant daycare $2,800-4,000/mo (Care.com NYC 2024 survey).
# 1BR to 2BR in NYC: $800-1,200/mo rent increase (StreetEasy 2024).
# Fertility data: at 32, ~85-90% cumulative pregnancy rate within 12 months of trying.
# At 34, ~78-80%. IVF success rate age 32: ~40-45% per cycle. Age 35+: ~30-35% per cycle.
# (CDC ART national summary 2022, SART data.)

My husband James and I have been circling this question for about a year. We keep saying "we'll decide soon" and then not deciding. So I'm writing this out.

I'm 32, he's 33. We've been married four years. We live in a 1BR in Brooklyn (Crown Heights). We both want kids — that's not the question. The question is whether we try now or wait about two years.

**The case for waiting**

I'm 18 months away from what looks like a fairly certain promotion to VP of Product at my company. My manager has said explicitly that I'm "on track" and the organizational structure supports it. The timing matters because VP-level at my company comes with significantly better equity refresh grants and a base jump from $175K to approximately $220-230K. That's $45-55K more per year. The equity component is meaningful — we're Series D and there's a path to liquidity in the next 3-4 years.

If I go on leave during the 18-month runway to the VP role, I'm not sure what that does to the promotion timeline. It probably delays it. Officially it wouldn't, and legally my company can't hold it against me, but realistically the visibility matters and the projects I'm running now are what my case is built on.

Two years also gives us time to save more money. We have about $55K in savings right now. By waiting two years we'd have closer to $110-120K depending on expenses. That matters because childcare in NYC is genuinely shocking — I've been told to expect $3,200-3,800/month for infant care, which is basically another rent payment. And we'd almost certainly need to move from our 1BR to a 2BR, which is probably $900-1,000 more per month in our neighborhood.

So the financial cost of a baby in year one is something like:
- Childcare: $3,500/month = $42,000/year
- Rent increase: $950/month = $11,400/year
- All the other stuff: probably $8-10K/year

Call it $60,000/year in new costs. That's real money and waiting two years to accumulate more savings and get the promotion first makes genuine sense.

**The case for not waiting**

I keep coming back to a few things.

The first is that we both want more than one child. If we wait two years to start, I'm 34 when we have the first. If we want a second child two to three years after that, I'm 36 or 37. I've read enough to know that fertility doesn't fall off a cliff at 35, but it does change. IVF success rates drop meaningfully between 32 and 37. The honest answer is that waiting two years compresses everything if we want two kids.

The second is that the "promotion first" logic has a flaw I can see but keep setting aside. What if we wait two years and something changes at the company? What if I get the promotion and then something happens that makes me want to leave anyway? I'm building my timing around an organizational outcome I don't fully control.

The third thing is harder to articulate. There's a version of this where we just keep waiting. When the timing is "right." And I'm aware of couples who did that, and some of them are fine and some of them are not.

**What I notice about how I'm thinking about this**

I frame "wait" as the responsible, sensible choice and "now" as something that needs justification. But I'm not sure that framing is neutral. Waiting feels controlled and deliberate. Having a baby now feels like giving something up or accepting disruption. But the disruption is coming either way — in two years or now.

James is more "now" than I am. He thinks we'll figure it out, that we always do, and that the window we're in right now at 32 and 33 is good. He's not wrong that financially we could manage it — tight for a year, then okay once the childcare costs decrease as kids get older. He also thinks I'm slightly overweighting the VP promotion as a fixed thing with a fixed date, when actually careers are flexible and I could have other opportunities.

**Where I actually am**

I don't know. I genuinely don't know. The financial case for waiting is solid. The career case for waiting is real but not certain. The fertility case for not waiting is real but not urgent at 32. The emotional case — I don't entirely trust my emotional read right now because I've been inside this question for so long that it's hard to see it clearly.

I think I want to wait. But I notice I can't fully articulate what I'm waiting for.
