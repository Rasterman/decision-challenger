# Should We Buy the House on Clearwater Drive?

We've been going back and forth on this for three months and I think we need to just write it out and make a decision.

The basics: we found a 4BR/2BA in Ahwatukee for $289,000. We've been renting a 2BR apartment for $1,100/month for the past four years and with a baby on the way it's not going to work anymore. The sellers are at $295K and we think we can get them to $289K or maybe $285K.

**Why the numbers look right to us**

Phoenix has been on an incredible run. Median home prices here went from around $130K in 2002 to roughly $260K by the end of 2005 — basically doubling in three years. The real estate agent showed us comps and the Ahwatukee market specifically has been strong. Even if you assume appreciation slows down significantly from the recent pace, holding for 5+ years should mean we come out ahead versus renting.

At $289K with 10% down, we're looking at a $260,100 30-year fixed mortgage. The rate we're pre-approved for is 6.25%, which puts our PITI at roughly $2,100/month. That's almost double our current rent, but we're also getting a lot more space and building equity instead of paying someone else's mortgage.

The loan officer also quoted us an ARM — 5/1 at 4.75% with a 2% annual cap and 5% lifetime cap. That would get us to around $1,750/month for the first five years. We're leaning toward the fixed because we're planning to stay long-term and we don't love the uncertainty, but we keep coming back to how much lower the monthly payment would be.

**Our income situation**

Combined we're at $142,000 — I'm a civil engineer at a firm that does municipal infrastructure contracts ($84K), and my wife works in hospital administration at Banner Health ($58K). We've been saving aggressively and have $45K in the down payment fund. Emergency fund is about $22K separate from that.

Here's the part that makes us feel okay about the payment: even if one of us loses a job, we can cover the mortgage on a single income. At $2,100/month on my $84K salary, that's 30% of gross — high but manageable. On her $58K salary it would be tight (43% of gross) but we could do it for a few months while the other one found something. Phoenix's job market has been strong — the population grew by about 45,000 people last year alone, which keeps driving demand for municipal contracts and healthcare services.

**What concerns us**

The price appreciation has been fast. We know that. The thing that's nagging me is whether we're buying near a peak. But honestly, every year for the past four years people have said the Phoenix market is overheated and every year it went up more. If we wait another year and prices climb another 8-10%, we've missed even more.

The risk I do take seriously is a housing dip. If prices fall 10-15% after we buy, that would be painful on paper, but we plan to hold for at least five to seven years, so any temporary dip resolves before we'd need to sell. We're not speculators flipping houses — we need a place to live and we're going to live there regardless of what the market does year to year.

The other risk is job loss. But as I laid out above, the single-income scenario works for either of us at the 30-year fixed rate, and Phoenix's economy has enough diversification (healthcare, construction, government, some tech) that both of us losing work at the same time is a very remote scenario.

**What we're telling ourselves**

We've been waiting for the "right time" for four years and we have a baby coming in October. The apartment won't work. Even if we're buying at a slight premium relative to some hypothetical future correction, we're buying a home we plan to live in for a long time, and the alternative is renting for $1,100 with no equity accumulation and another lease renewal in January.

We're going to make an offer this week. Writing this out actually helped. The math works, we can handle the payments, we have a cushion, and we're buying for the right reasons.
